# Weekly report notes

Moved word for word from the root CLAUDE.md in the Phase 6b restructure; nothing in it was edited.

## The weekly report's inputs (verified 2026-10-09)

**What an analyst does, for any week ending on a Friday:** upload that week's files on the Fixed Income or Equities start screen and type the analysts' own figures there if no workbook was sent, draft the five sections, review and approve each, export the Word file.

### This week's inputs (`weekly/inputs.py`, `/api/inputs/weekly/{week_ending}`, `frontend/.../InputsPanel.vue`)

The workbooks live on the analysts' machines; each Friday they send them and the analyst uploads them. The panel has a week-ending Friday picker (choosing another Friday switches the report to `Week ending <date>`) and one slot per input: the equities workbook, the fixed income workbook, KCB IB's daily trading reports Monday to Friday, the CBK Weekly Bulletin (a "Fetch from CBK" button, or an upload) and, optionally, the NSE yield curve. Each slot shows received or not, **the date read from the file itself**, and whether that is this week. **KCB IB's weekly report slot is hidden (2026-11-25, `SHOW_KCB_WEEKLY = False` in `weekly/inputs.py`; the team does not use it for the Weekly):** it is left out of `status()`, so of the panel, readiness, the agent tools and the review's fix-by-upload links, and is never required; its upload route and every reader stay, so a weekly report already stored is read as before. It also takes the analysts' T-bond bidding range and, optionally, the previous issue's address.

- **Storage:** `<CYTONN_DATA_DIR>/inputs/weekly/<week-ending date>/` (`manifest.json`, `notes.json` beside the files), never inside the repository tree; OCR readings are cached in `<CYTONN_DATA_DIR>/inputs/ocr_cache/`. `.gitignore` ignores `backend/data/` whole and `sample data/`.
- **An upload is the request body itself** (`PUT /api/inputs/weekly/{week}/{slot}?filename=`), so no multipart dependency. It is checked before it is kept: size (30 MB), kind by its first bytes (never its name), and that it parses as the slot's document. A KCB daily report is filed under the date printed in it and refused if that day is outside the week; KCB's weekly report in the daily slot is refused; a workbook for another week is stored and shown as not this week's.
- **A workbook for another week covers nothing (2026-11-24, untested at commit).** Each workbook's own date is the one `identify` reads: the equities workbook's newest dated row with a P/E on 'NASI-PE & Dividend Yield', the fixed income workbook's fund table title date (the Friday, or the as-at day of a holiday week, as `read_mmf_table` takes it). When it is not this week's, or none can be read, the slot's readiness line names the week found and the report's week (or says no date can be read), the slot counts as missing, `Analyst.book_stale()` puts a `workbook_stale` (`whole: true`) on every form item the workbook holds, so readiness counts none of them as covered and a carried value it holds still waits for confirmation, and the draft's warnings say so (`stale_lines`). **What the draft does with it is split by direction (decided 2026-11-24):** a workbook whose date falls in an **earlier week, or that has no readable date**, is never opened by a draft (`WeeklyInputs.earlier()`; `fi_book` and `equities_book` return None, so no figure from it reaches any part, not even as a second reading), each part that needed it says "the uploaded ... is for the week ending X, an earlier week, so the draft does not read it" (`Analyst.unread()`), and the warning names both weeks; one for a **later week** is still read (a deliberate later workbook, `test_weekly_equities.py`'s Universe of Coverage golden), with each value flagged in the warnings. A date inside the report week that is not a report date counts as neither, so it is read and warned. The form and readiness follow the draft's rule (2026-11-25): `analyst.status` opens the workbooks as a draft does, so an earlier or undated one gives no form item a `workbook`, `workbook_rows` or `workbook_ref` (nothing folds as "from the workbook"), `workbooks` reports it as absent, and only the slot and the draft's warnings say why; a later one is shown with its `workbook_stale`. Only the rows export opens it whatever its date (`WeeklyInputs(..., old_books=True)`), for its layout. Nothing counts a later workbook as this week's when its readers find the week's own rows; the per-reader date checks only refuse readings. The readiness tool (`week_status`, read by the chat model) lists each kept file by its stored name only; `GET /api/sections/{slug}/readiness` adds the analyst's own file name for the prepare page's Remove dialog. Tests: `test_workbook_week.py`.
- **Hidden sheets (2026-11-16).** `Workbook.sheet_state` is available in read-only mode, so a value read from a hidden or very hidden sheet is recorded (`Workbook.hidden_lines()`, read by `WeeklyInputs.hidden_sheet_lines()`) and each such sheet is one line in the section's warnings ("<workbook>: values were read from the hidden sheet '<name>'. Check that the team means that sheet to be used."; very hidden adds that Excel's Unhide menu does not list it), in the weekly Fixed Income and Equities and, through `read_dp_workbook`, the Digital Payments workbook's warnings. It changes no figure and no item's status. **Hidden rows and columns are not detected:** read-only mode has no `row_dimensions` or `column_dimensions`, so finding them needs a second, non-read-only open (measured 2026-11-16: 25 ms against 33 ms on the synthetic workbooks, and 1.1 s against 1.6 s for a 1,000-row, 10-sheet workbook, 5.1 s against 8.5 s at 5,000 rows); not switched on. Tests: `test_workbook_hidden_sheets.py`.
- **Nothing is guessed.** A reader finds its cell by sheet name plus a label or a date, never a fixed row (`weekly/workbook.py`), and says what is missing in the analyst's terms: "'NASI-PE & Dividend Yield' has no row for 2026-10-09: workbook not yet updated for this week". A missing input is never an error: the part that needed it becomes an `unavailable` block naming it.
- **Confidentiality.** The workbooks, KCB's reports and figures that come only from them (target prices, DPS, EPS, the P/E series, the fund table, bank placements, the turnover series) are never committed, in code, tests or docs.

### Weekly analyst inputs (`weekly/analyst.py`, `/api/inputs/weekly/{week_ending}/analyst`, `frontend/.../analyst/AnalystPanel.vue`; built 2026-10-12)

The team may not send the two workbooks every week (Clive's decision, 2026-10-12), so every figure the weekly reads **only** from a workbook can be typed instead, on a second panel under "This week's inputs" on the Fixed Income and Equities start screens. The workbook upload stays as an optional shortcut.

- **What is on the form** (audited from the builders; nothing the tool reads from CBK, KCB IB, the NSE or the previous issue is on it). Fixed Income: 3-month bank placements; the Cytonn fund's yield the previous week; the money market fund table, the date it was published and the source label printed under it; the daily interbank rates; an optional one-line "What drove liquidity this week" (`liquidity_driver`, at most 200 characters, the team's own words, appended to the Liquidity paragraph's first sentence and noted as analyst input; not carried forward; the tool never writes a cause itself); the Eurobond issues (amount issued, maturity date, yield at issue); the Eurobond yields on the year's and the month's first trading day; monthly remittances; the net domestic borrowing target, the day the fiscal year starts (1 July when empty; carried forward), bond redemptions CBK prints on no result file ("Other bond redemptions") and, optionally, an auction log that overrides CBK's (see **Net domestic borrowing log** in backend/src/cytonn_weekly/fixed_income/CLAUDE.md). Equities: the large-cap list; the year-to-date turnover; the foreign net position year to date, in the previous year and by week; the market P/E and dividend yield and their historical averages; the Universe of Coverage inputs per company (four prices, target price, DPS, shares, tangible book value) and the date of the month-end price.
- **Not on the form, because the workbook only cross-checks them:** the T-bill figures, the daily US dollar rates, the reserves, the interbank volumes and the index levels. Without a workbook those cross-checks are simply absent: an OCR'd NSE 10 or Banking index level then has no second reading and is flagged, where the workbook's typed level would have made it not auto-verified.
- **Storage:** `<CYTONN_DATA_DIR>/inputs/weekly/<week-ending date>/analyst.json`, per reporting week, values kept as typed (a percentage as 9.0). `FIELDS` and `TABLES` in `weekly/analyst.py` are the one definition of each item: label, unit, valid range, source label ("Daily Nation e-paper", "Bloomberg NASI P/E"), the part that prints it. The API serves that definition and the screen renders it; the screen holds no field list.
- **Checked on save, all or nothing.** `PUT .../analyst` takes `{fields, tables}`; a table is `{"text": pasted lines}` or `{"rows": [...]}`. Any refused value is a 422 listing every reason, one a line, and nothing is stored. Numbers must be in range (a percentage typed as 0.09 is refused with "Type a percentage as 9.0"); the fund table's ranks must run 1, 2, 3 with none skipped, its funds must be unique and at least five (a higher rate lower down is kept, with a warning); interbank days must be weekdays of the two weeks compared; remittance months must run with none skipped; a redemption is negative; the year-open and month-open Eurobond rows must be dated in the first week of January and of the month.
- **The fund table is a paste box, any number of rows** (`parse_paste`): a line is cut on tabs, else on spaces, and the fund's name takes what is left between the rank and the rate. A line with no digit is a heading. Nothing assumes 34 funds.
- **Derived figures are never typed.** The Top 5 average, the pro-rated borrowing target, and the Universe of Coverage's changes, yield, upside, P/TBV and recommendation come from the builders' formulas and are worked out again by the checker.
- **How a builder reads it:** `inputs.analyst` (an `Analyst`) returns, per item, the typed value as a `Typed` cell, else the uploaded workbook's `Cell`, else nothing. A `Typed` cell stands where a workbook cell would, in the workbook's unit, so the arithmetic is the same whichever the source (tested: typing a workbook's figures gives the same inputs, figures, text and tables). `prose.typed()` words its source "analyst input typed on the weekly form (<item>; source: <label>)", so it is an analyst input everywhere a workbook cell is: not auto-verified, never clean.
- **A typed value overrides the workbook, and a difference is shown.** The workbook's cell rides on the `Typed` cell as its second reading (a "sources disagree" flag on the figure, or on the table row through `second_sources`), and every difference is also a line in the section's warnings ("Weekly analyst inputs: ... typed as 9.75%, but the uploaded fixed income workbook has 9.5% ... The typed value is used."). A pasted table replaces the workbook's table whole; rows on one side only are named. **The fund table is the one exception (2026-11-24, untested at commit):** with `MMF_WORKBOOK_FIRST = True` (top of `weekly/analyst.py`) an accepted workbook fund table is used and a form table, pasted or confirmed from a Daily Nation snip, is its second reading (each differing rate a `Booked` cell carrying the form's value; the differences are worded the other way round, "... The workbook's value is used."); the workbook's date goes with its table. False restores the rule above. Every other item keeps "typed wins". The table's `source` carries `via` ("rows from the fixed income workbook (...)", "rows read from the Daily Nation snip and confirmed", "rows typed on the weekly form"), shown on the review screen only; the Word source line still prints the name alone.
- **A sheet for another week is said, not hidden (2026-11-24).** `fi_workbook.read_mmf_table` raises `WorkbookWeekError` (the date found and the dates expected) when the fund table's title is not dated this week's Friday or as-at day, and `read_placements` and `read_mmf_last_week` refuse the same way when given the week, since the fund table's title is the only date on 'Money Market Performance'. `Analyst.stale` keeps each; the form item carries it as `workbook_stale` (shown even when a value is typed), the readiness check words it from the dates ("the workbook holds it, but dated ...; this week needs ..."), and both weekly builders add `Analyst.stale_lines()` to the section's warnings. The form's chips (`lib/analyst.ts`) add "From the workbook, ...; the form's value is its second reading", "In the workbook, but for another week" and ", also in the workbook" beside a typed value the workbook holds too.
- **Nothing is filled in.** An item in neither place is missing: `Analyst.why()` words the `unavailable` block, naming the items ("The money market fund table has not been typed under "Weekly analyst inputs", and the fixed income workbook for this week is not among this week's inputs."). Typed weekly foreign positions that stop before the last change of direction give the sentence without a streak, never a guessed count.
- **The screen** shows each item's state by chip (Typed / Typed, differs from the workbook / From the workbook / Empty), the workbook's value as the placeholder of an empty field, and the rows in use under a table. A field saves when the cursor leaves it; a table has its own Save. While anything typed is unsaved or refused, the draft button is disabled, so a draft never starts on a form the API does not hold.
- **The fund table's source label** (`mmf_source`, 2026-10-30): a short text item that prints on the fund table's source line. Empty or missing it is "Daily Nation" (`MMF_SOURCE_DEFAULT`), so an older `analyst.json` keeps working; blank after trimming clears it; over 60 characters or any line break is refused. It is a label, not a figure: it carries forward and is used at once. The source is the Daily Nation e-paper (Ryan, 19 October), so the default stands. Since 26 Nov 2026 (Ryan: Daily Nation, the Friday issue; Business Daily or the funds' own websites only when it is missed there) the field is `required=False`, so the prepare page lists it under the defaults in use with Override; the default is never written to the form, the carry or the history store, and the review's `origin_note` on `mmf_table` says when it was used.
- **"Yields at Issue":** a typed yield replaces the `YIELDS_AT_ISSUE` figure for that issue; an issue with none typed prints the stored figure (see Open items in CLAUDE.md).
- **Prices in the Universe of Coverage (2026-10-12):** this Friday's price is KCB IB's, from its weekly report (`COVERAGE_TICKERS` maps the thirteen coverage names to KCB's tickers; the report's price equals the workbook's for all thirteen in the 2 October sample, pinned by a test that skips without the samples). A typed price overrides it and a difference is flagged; on a workbook row KCB's price is the second reading of the workbook's. Last Friday's price is the Friday price stored for the previous week (typed, else KCB's, else that week's workbook), named as such in the row's note. The month-end price and Year Open are typed; the month-end price carries forward only while the month-end date does.
- **Start from last week's form** (`carry_forward`, `POST .../analyst/carry`). It copies what last week's form holds, confirmed, into every item still empty this week, and marks each with the week it came from (`carried` in `analyst.json`; the screen shows "Carried from week ending ..." while the value is still the carried one, cell by cell in a grid). Slow-changing items carry and are used at once: the fund table's source label, the large-cap list, the previous year's foreign position (same year only), the month-end date (same month only), the net domestic borrowing target, the Eurobond issues and base-day yields (while still valid), remittances, and the Universe of Coverage's Year Open, target, DPS, shares and tangible book. Weekly-changing items carry **unconfirmed**: placements, the fund table, the P/E and dividend yield with their averages, the auction log, the interbank days still in the window, the weekly foreign positions. **An unconfirmed value is read by no builder** (`usable_form`), so a draft cannot rest on last week's figure by oversight; the part that needs it is unavailable and says "carried from the week ending ... and has not been confirmed". Confirming (`PUT .../analyst` with `confirm: [keys]`) or typing over it makes it this week's, and the draft button is disabled while anything waits. Never carried: the fund table's date, the running totals, the prices. The Cytonn fund's "previous week" yield is taken from last week's fund table.
- **Suggested running totals** (`equities/weekly.running_total_suggestions`, `POST .../analyst/suggest/{field}`): for the year-to-date turnover and foreign position, last week's saved total plus this week's figure as the section itself works it out from KCB IB and CBK (no OCR reading), to 4 decimals, with the working stored and shown. It is kept unconfirmed like a carried weekly value, never replaces a typed or confirmed one, and is refused (409, with the reason) when last week has no total (or holds one never confirmed), a day has no figure, or the year turned; each reason ends with what to do instead, and the screen shows it under that field's button. Last week's total is its confirmed typed value, else its uploaded equities workbook's. `create_app(fetch_usd_rates=...)` is how tests keep it off the network.
- **Fund names:** `fund_key` treats any name starting "Cytonn Money Market Fund" as the one fund, so the paper's name matches the workbook's longer one. No other fund is aliased.
- **A single OCR reading says what to check** (`scanned(check=...)`, quoted by the flag): the NSE price list's own address for that day and the figure to read in it, or the equities team's figure. Index levels stay off the form.
- The bidding range is unchanged: typed text, never suggested.

### Parsers (`weekly/`)

- **`kcb.py`**: KCB IB's daily and weekly trading reports (one-page PDFs with a text layer). **They cover NSE equities only**: no T-bills, money market, Eurobonds or shilling. Read by printed label: the date under the title; the indicators (NASI, NSE-20, NSE-25, market cap, shares, "Equities TO", foreign buy, sell, net) for the two dates in the header row; TRADING STATS' Equities row; each security's price and change; on the weekly report, DAILY FOREIGN FLOWS by weekday. The equities turnover is the **TRADING STATS Equities row**, not the "Equities TO" indicator, which includes ETFs on the weekly report. The text layer kerns digits apart ("1 2,327,385"), so `split_numbers` accepts a row only when there is exactly one way to cut its tokens into the expected count of well-formed numbers (no leading zeros; one indicator's two figures share their decimals), and flows are checked arithmetically to within the report's whole-shilling rounding.
- **`cbk_bulletin.py`**: the CBK Weekly Bulletin, published on a Friday and covering the Friday before to the Thursday before. Table 1 exchange rates; Table 2 reserves and months of import cover; Table 3 interbank deals, value and KESONIA with a weekly average row; Table 4 T-bill auctions (this week's and the previous week's side by side); Table 5 T-bond auctions as printed; Table 6 daily NASI, NSE 25, NSE 20, equity turnover, market cap, bond turnover and Eurobond yields, each yield column matched to the maturity year printed above it by position; and the "Government Domestic Debt" table's bond stock, found by its title. **Each table is read only from its own title down** to the next "Source:", "Table N:" or "Chart N:" line, never from the whole page: CBK moves charts between pages (in the 9 October 2026 issue the yield curve chart sits above Table 6, and its legend "28-Jun-24 30-Jun-25 2-Oct-26" starts with a date like a row). **Each table is read on its own** (2026-10-13): one that cannot be read or fails its checks is left out whole, so nothing of it is partly used, and is named in `Bulletin.unavailable` (and `problems`, which the inputs panel shows as the file's note) with the reason, the issue date and the file's sha256; the other tables still load, and only a PDF with no "Table 1:" heading is refused as not the bulletin. Fixed Income and Equities put each table left out in the section's warnings, and a part that needed it gives the table's refusal as its reason. Table 5's rows are found by the words of their labels alone, because the labels wrap differently from issue to issue. **It is fetched by date**: the listing page (`/weekly-bulletin/`) links every issue under `/uploads/weekly_bulletin/` with the issue date in the file name, written several ways.
- **`cbk_rates.py`**: CBK's daily US dollar rate, fetched from the data table behind the Forex page (`admin-ajax.php?action=get_wdtable&table_id=193`, a form POST answering JSON). It is current to the day and holds about two years. The older table on `/rates/forex-exchange-rates/` and the "historical data" CSV both stop in January 2024.
- **`nse_price_list.py`**: the NSE daily equity price list, `https://www.nse.co.ke/wp-content/uploads/DD-MON-YY.pdf`, a scanned image with no text layer. Its index box is the only public source of the **NSE 10 and the Banking Sector index**. **OCR: `rapidocr-onnxruntime`** (pinned in `pyproject.toml`): it installs with pip alone on Windows and in the image, with its models inside the wheel; Tesseract needs a separate program and EasyOCR pulls in PyTorch and downloads models. In Docker its OpenCV dependency needs `libgl1` and `libglib2.0-0`, which `backend/Dockerfile` installs (the image has not been rebuilt and run since). OCR is never trusted alone: a whole-page pass finds the box, the box is cut out and read twice (at the scan's size and enlarged), and a figure is kept only if both readings agree. A reading takes about half a minute and is cached per PDF.
- **`nse_yield_curve.py`**: the NSE yield curve PDF (91 and 182 days, 1 to 29 years), interpolated linearly between whole years and never extrapolated.
- **`equities_workbook.py`**, **`fi_workbook.py`**: the two workbooks (sheet names verbatim, two with a trailing space). What each reads is in its docstring.
- **`previous_issue.py`**: the previous issue on cytonnreport.com. There is no listing endpoint: `/series/1` links the newest issue, `/api/global/search/<text>` resolves a page name to an id, and ids are walked down (the newest `MAX_WALK` = 12 only) to the newest issue published on or before the week's Friday. A pasted address or id overrides it and is never filtered. **Stripped twins are skipped (2026-10-15).** The site also holds copies of an issue with the charts removed, and the stripped copy can have the higher id (850 for 849, 889 for 888, 841 for 840; read from the 53 issues saved as ids 840 to 892). A candidate is passed over only if BOTH hold: its Fixed Income and Equities sections together hold fewer than `STRIPPED_BELOW_IMAGES` (4) images (every full weekly of 2026 has at least 6 in Fixed Income and 2 in Equities; the stripped copies have 0 or 1), AND an issue with more images in those two sections was published within `TWIN_WINDOW_DAYS` (6) days before it (the stripped copies came 0 to 5 days after the full one, and a weekly comes every 7 days, so a fuller issue 7 days earlier is last week's, not a twin). A stripped candidate with no such twin is returned as before: a genuine image-light issue is kept, and the walk never falls back to an older full issue for it. The look-ahead for a twin stays inside the same 12 ids. A candidate with neither section (a companion-only issue) counts as image-light, so it is skipped too if a weekly was published within 6 days before it. **A full Markets Review is never skipped, but a stripped copy of a review is skipped like any other stripped copy** (841, the stripped FY'2025 review, is passed over for 840): a full review is the genuinely previous issue (the week ending 9 Oct 2026 returns 892, the Q3 review, by design and by test; the shilling lookup falls back to its Kenya Macro section). When a review supplies the carried text the flag says so (see **Computed paragraphs** in backend/src/cytonn_weekly/review/CLAUDE.md). `Issue.kind` (`weekly` or `review`) and `Issue.skipped_twins` carry what was done; the carried block says it (see **Computed paragraphs** in backend/src/cytonn_weekly/review/CLAUDE.md). Replayed on 2026-10-15 over 37 weeks (every 2026 Friday that has a published weekly, 35, and the weeks ending 2 and 9 October) against the saved issues: exactly three weeks changed (9 Jan 841 to 840, 27 Feb 850 to 849, 25 Sep 889 to 888) and 34 did not. The week ending 27 Feb now finds the Fixed Income outlook (850 has none); the week ending 9 Jan still has no shilling text, because 840 is also a review and holds no such paragraph. Five weeks still return a review (9 Jan, 23 Jan, 10 Apr, 10 Jul, 9 Oct), which is the design, not a twin.

### Week windows

Equities, turnover and foreign flows: Monday to Friday. The shilling: Friday to Friday. The interbank market: Friday to Thursday (the bulletin's weekly row). Eurobond yields: the last six trading days up to Thursday. T-bills: the week's Thursday auction, whose value date is the following Monday (a workbook is this week's when its value date falls in the seven days after the Friday). The year-to-date base everywhere is the year's first trading day, read from the source (`weekly/week.py`).

**Holiday weeks (2026-11-05).** A week is still named by its Friday (`Week ending 2026-12-25` is a valid period; a day that is not a Friday is still refused), even when that Friday is a market holiday. `Week.as_at` is the last trading day Monday to Friday, `previous_as_at` the previous week's (Christmas week 2026: Thu 24 Dec against Fri 18 Dec; the week ending 1 Jan 2027: Thu 31 Dec against Thu 24 Dec), and `trading_days` is Monday to the as-at day without known closures. Closures are `backend/src/cytonn_weekly/weekly/market_closures.json` (ISO date to name, read on every use; holds only 25 Dec 2026 and 1 Jan 2027, the team's answer, 2026-11-05). The analyst can set another as-at day on the start screen (the week's `notes.json`, key `as_at`; `WeeklyInputs.week` carries it). Every Equities and Fixed Income price, rate, workbook-row and "Price as at" header reads `as_at`/`previous_as_at`; the year of a year-to-date base is the as-at day's year (the week ending 1 Jan 2027 is still in 2026's running totals, as the week ending 2 Jan 2026 was in 2025's); a Monday-to-Friday sum notes how many trading days it used. A document dated the Friday or the as-at day is this week's (`Week.report_dates`), and the CBK bulletin is asked for by both dates. Each holiday week leads the Equities and Fixed Income warnings with "Friday <date> was a market holiday; figures are as at <as-at date>" and shows it on the inputs panel. Digital Payments' table is fetched as at the as-at day (`as_at` on its builder); Real Estate already walks back from the Friday. Not holiday-safe: the Thursday-based windows (interbank, Eurobonds) assume a Thursday that is open, and the bond-stock check pairs only Friday-dated bulletin columns, so it skips a holiday week's interval. The chart reader's freshness check compares the newest workbook row with the as-at day.

### Not repeating the previous issue (2026-11-17; `previous_issue.digest`, `drafting/drafter.draft_pieces(earlier=...)`)

The weekly Fixed Income and Equities highlights read the previous issue's same section (`previous_issue.digest(issue, "fixed-income" | "equities")`: its bold headings and the address of every link, compared by `previous_issue.norm_url`: no scheme, no `www.`, no fragment, no trailing slash). The headings go into each brief's context as "Already reported in the previous issue, do not repeat: ...". After a piece is drafted, in code and not in the prompt, a piece with a claim that cites an address the previous issue's section already linked is dropped: it is not counted toward the target (the next brief is tried), `skipped` carries "already reported in the previous issue", the draft warns and a `part_skipped` run event names the address. When the previous issue cannot be read (`issue` is None) the digest is None and nothing changes. Same page is judged by address, not by event: a new article on the same event is kept.

### Section summaries (`weekly/summaries.py`)

On cytonnreport.com every section has a body and a summary (1,500 to 3,000 characters of its own lead paragraphs), and the website builds the Executive Summary from the summaries. A summary here is composed, never drafted: Fixed Income and Equities compose theirs from their computed lead paragraphs; the drafted sections' is the opening paragraph of each piece, never cut inside a paragraph (a Focus of findings has none: the analyst writes it); `serialize_review` returns it as `summary`.
### Export (`weekly/export.py`, `GET`/`POST /api/export/weekly?period=`, `frontend/.../ExportBar.vue`)

"Export weekly report" on the weekly overview writes the approved week as one `.docx` and downloads it. **It is enabled only when every weekly section of that report and period is approved**: the API answers 409 naming each section that is not ("Fixed Income (still in review); Equities (not drafted); ..."), and writes nothing. The file is kept under `<CYTONN_DATA_DIR>/exports/weekly/`.

- The five sections under the issue's own headings and in its order (Fixed Income, Equities, Real Estate, Digital Payments Weekly Highlights, Focus of the Week); every table as a Word table with its source line and footnotes; the Eurobond table as the issue prints it (issues across, the fixed rows on top), built from the checked display strings (`export_grid`).
- Each chart of the real issue after the part the issue puts it under (`chart_after` in the section content): the tool's own picture for the two NASI charts when it drew them and the analyst did not set them aside, otherwise `[Chart to add: <caption>]` for the team's own chart (see **Charts the tool draws** in backend/src/cytonn_weekly/charts/CLAUDE.md). The tool draws no other chart.
- Carried-forward text under a red `[Carried forward from the previous issue: edit before publishing]` line, so it never passes for finished copy. ## Fetched form tables (Phase 5, 2026-11-18: `fetched_items.py`, `cbk_kesonia.py`, `cbk_remittances.py`)

- **`POST /api/inputs/weekly/{week}/analyst/fetch/{key}`** fetches one form table from CBK (`FETCHERS`: `interbank`, `remittances`) and keeps it on the form as rows marked `carried.from == "fetched"` with a note (source, address, day fetched, sha256 of what was read). It is not "unconfirmed": a draft reads it at once, and the form shows "fetched from CBK". It never replaces rows someone typed (409); rows carried, suggested or fetched earlier are replaced. Editing a fetched row makes the table typed (the mark no longer matches). The chat groups an unedited fetched table under `fetched` (`agent/readiness._item`).
- **Interbank:** CBK's old interbank table (wpDataTable 12) stops on 29 Aug 2025; its successor is the KESONIA table (id 194, `/kesonia/`, same admin-ajax call, 4 dp, current to the day, from 1 Sep 2025). Source label "Central Bank of Kenya, KESONIA". All 20 days of the saved 2 and 9 Oct bulletins equal it at 2 dp. Every trading day of the two weeks compared must be in the table, else "no rate yet for ..." (404) and nothing is kept.
- **Remittances:** the diaspora page holds the whole table as static HTML (wpDataTable 189, USD '000, 2004-01 to the latest month). The header names and the unit text are checked; the latest 24 months up to the week's month must be in a row. Values are the exact quotient in USD mn (up to 5 dp, not rounded to the form's 1 dp). A North America cell CBK prints as 0.00 is left empty.
- **Failures** are 404 (not published yet) or 502 (site down, page not as expected), each worded "... Type them under 'Weekly analyst inputs' instead", code `item_fetch_failed`; nothing is partly kept. Tests: `test_fetched_interbank.py`, `test_fetched_remittances.py`, fixtures `cbk_kesonia_captured_2026-11-18.json` and `cbk_diaspora_remittances_captured_2026-11-18.html` (public figures, cut small).

- **Eurobond openings (`cbk_eurobond_opens.py`, 2026-11-18, step 3b the same day):** the form's two fixed rows (the year's and the month's first day) are fetched from the archived CBK Weekly Bulletins (`FETCHERS['eurobond_yields']`). The table is read by the weekly parser's own reader, `cbk_bulletin.table_6_rows`, found by its title (its number moves: Table 7 in the January 2026 issue, 6 since). Known layouts only: five yield columns (13 figures a row), eight (16) or nine (17, from 6 Nov 2026, with a 2033 bond), each yield matched to the maturity year printed over it by position; a "Public Holiday" row carries the yields alone. The one year read and ignored is "2008", the NASI base ("100=2008"), which the 6 Nov text layer splits off as a word of its own over the NASI column, far left of every yield column. Any other layout, an extra or missing figure, a non-number cell, another year outside the columns, a yield under no single year, or a year list that lacks the five form issues refuses the whole bulletin. `opening` takes the first Friday issue (the 2nd to the 14th, not after the week) that is in the archive, reads, and prints a day before the 1st and every weekday from there to the day it takes (the 6 Nov 2026 issue prints 2 to 5 Oct then 23 to 30 Oct, so contiguity is checked); its first printed day of the month counts (year: on or before the 8th, month: the 7th). The year's opening skips 1 January (markets are shut and CBK carries the yields forward, 4 of 5 equal Dec 31's), a month's first day does not (1 Jun is a real trading day). No qualifying issue means "type it" (404, nothing kept).

**The Daily Nation fund table (2026-11-18, Phase 5 checkpoint 4; `fund_snip.py`, routes `.../fund-snip*` and `/api/settings/fund-list*` in `api/routers/inputs.py`).** The money market fund table is read from the analyst's upload of the Daily Nation Unit Trusts page (PNG or JPEG, up to 10 MB, one image a week; it stays in the gitignored data folder under `inputs/weekly/<Friday>/fund_snip/`, named by its sha256, which is the source reference). The page is never committed and never in a test; tests use invented names and a fake reader (`create_app(read_fund_snip=...)`).
- **Reading:** the page's currency column (Kenya Shilling or USD) finds the Unit Trusts rows and the names' left edge, the panel is cut out and read twice by RapidOCR (scales 2.0 and 3.0, `rapidocr_engine` and `_enlarge` from `nse_price_list.py`), and a cell is kept only when both readings agree; any other cell is unresolved and named. The rate is the right-most figure (the Annual Rate column; the Daily Yield is the one left of it). Unreadable pages end in a plain "type it or upload a clearer image", never a guess.
- **Classification (`classify`):** USD rows are a separate group outside the ranking (counted only, never asked about); a shilling row is `keep` when its name matches the fund list or a saved name match, `excluded` when the analyst left it out, `auto` when its name says Fixed Income, Special, Equity, Balanced or Bond (no prompt, shown in a collapsed "Left out" list), else `new` (the analyst puts it on the list, leaves it out, or says which list fund it is). Names are matched by `match_key` (letters and digits only, "MMF" as money market, no "fund", no currency tail). The list, the exclusions and the name matches are one setting for every week (`inputs/settings/fund_list.json`), loaded from a pasted file and edited on the card; `backend/scripts/seed_fund_list.py` writes the 34 names of the mastersheet's fund table, names only, to a text file outside the repo.
- **Confirming (`confirmed_rows`, `analyst.fund_snip`):** refused while any cell is unresolved, any fund is undecided, or one list fund appears twice; the table is ranked here by `rank_mmf_yields` (equal rates share a rank 1, 2, 2, 4), and the form's `mmf` check now accepts a repeated rank only when the rates are equal. A list fund the page does not carry can be typed (name and rate) so the table keeps its size; it ranks with the others. The published date is typed by the analyst (inside the week). On the 16 Oct 2026 sample page: 69 fund rows, 13 USD, 27 on the list, 25 left out by name, 4 to decide, and 7 of the 34 list funds not on the page (counts only).
- **Rank check against the last mastersheet (counts only):** recomputing the 34 printed ranks from its unrounded rates matches 33; the one difference is a tied pair (printed 8 and 9, the shared-rank rule gives 8 and 8). At 1 dp the rates tie in 11 pairs, which is why the form ranks on the 2 dp rates the page prints.
