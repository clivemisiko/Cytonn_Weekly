## Sources (per section)

- **Digital Payments** (`digital_payments/`):
  - **Stock table** (`fetcher.py`), from Yahoo Finance: 7 companies, American Express, Visa, Mastercard, Circle, Block, PayPal, Global Payments (tickers AXP, V, MA, CRCL, XYZ (not SQ), PYPL, GPN). No Wise Plc, despite the report's own intro text. It fetches current price, the price 7 days ago, and the report's "Year Open", which is the **close** of the year's first trading day (taken from the data, never a hard-coded date; field `ytd_open`), and YTD is computed from it. That is the rule that reproduces the printed Year Open and YTD columns of 16 issues for six of seven companies (the first day's open reproduced none); Global Payments is a known difference, see Open items in CLAUDE.md. The Q3 and H1 period tables share this base. Forward P/E is Yahoo's `forwardPE` when positive and finite, else `price / forwardEps`. That's the analyst-consensus basis, reverse-engineered from real issues, not FY2025 audited despite the report's footnote. Display: prices and P/E to 1 dp, `x` suffix on P/E, % to 1 dp, parentheses for negatives.
  - **Weekly Highlights** (`highlights.py`): up to 4. Visa, Mastercard, American Express and PayPal are always all searched first and each one that released appears; the slots left up to four are then filled from the other companies (Circle), so three priority stories plus Circle's print four (the team's rule, 5 Nov 2026, read per slot). A priority search that found but discarded a result gets a "could not be confirmed, check by hand" warning. Each company is searched on its IR domain first (investor.visa.com, investor.mastercard.com, ir.americanexpress.com, investor.pypl.com, investor.circle.com), then the open web on `NO_STORY`. Block and Global Payments are never highlighted, and a shortfall (fewer than four after filling) is reported, never backfilled. Each is a roman-numeral headline plus ~100-180 words opening "During the week, [Company] ...", with inline hyperlinked citations. The table is item V. `draft_outlook()` writes the closing ~80-120-word paragraph plus `stats`.
  - **Digital Payments workbook** (`digital_payments/workbook.py`, optional upload slot `dp_workbook` in the weekly inputs, 2026-11-05): read by company name, never by row. The workbook's own TTM and forward P/E formulas are followed with each Earnings reference moved to the company's own row in the same table (a company missing or listed twice is refused by name, at upload); a saved P/E or Average that differs by more than 0.05 from that is a reviewer warning ("... uses another company's earnings"), the Average is the seven named companies with a negative P/E kept in, a Year Open that is not the first trading day's close in the fetched rows is a warning, and a blank quarter is handled by the rule below. It changes no printed figure and the checker is untouched. **A blank is never zero (2026-11-05).** A blank Earnings cell that a company's TTM P/E formula reads is never counted as 0 and the sum is never shortened (Excel does both): it is recorded as a `Quarter` (company, quarter label, cell address) and that company's TTM P/E is unavailable, with the reason, in `DpRow.reason`, the section's warnings and `section["workbook_pe"]`. A figure is looked for in this order: the company's row on the Financial Results sheet (`fill_blanks`; matched by company name and the quarter in the row's title, "PayPal (Q3)" with the year from the column header); then a web search (`exa_search`, the same Exa path the local provider uses, limited to the company's investor relations domain from `HIGHLIGHT_COMPANIES`, where it has one, and sec.gov; injectable as `search`, default the real one), where fixed rules read the net income from the page text, the page must say it covers that quarter and year, a figure the backend claims must appear verbatim in the page text, more than one different figure refuses the page, and `check_figure` runs on it; the model supplies no number; else it stays unavailable with a plain reason (a failed search is a reason too, never an error). A company whose latest Earnings quarter (a found one counts) is older than its latest quarter on Financial Results gets a window warning naming both. The TTM Average is over the companies with an available P/E, and the warning names those left out (also when the workbook's own Average agrees). The structure of a published Focus (issue 895, #42.2026; confirmed by Ryan) is the analyst's to write; what the tool still does:

  - **Input.** The topic (500 characters) and an optional **Sections** box: up to 8 titles, one per line, at most 120 characters each, cleaned like the topic (whitespace normalized, a typed "Section N:" prefix dropped; nothing is cut). `clean_sections` is the one judge (both draft routes call it through `resolve_draft`, a 422 with a sentence such as "9 section titles; the limit is 8" and the `draft_input_invalid` problem); the web form counts the same way (`sectionTitles`, `sectionsProblem`, `MAX_SECTIONS`, `MAX_SECTION_TITLE` in `frontend/src/lib/topic.ts`). The tool numbers sections in Roman numerals in code ("Section I: <title>").
  - **Outline.** Blank Sections: one model call proposes 4 to 6 titles (titles only, parsed in code: a reply with prose, the wrong count, no overview first or no "Recommendations and Conclusion" last is refused with "Type the section titles yourself"). Typed titles are used exactly as typed and none is added.
  - **Findings, one call per section (not the closing one).** `section_brief` sets `NarrativeBrief.findings`: the prompt (`drafting.findings_system`) asks for no prose, with 5 to 12 findings each as a claim, and `piece_from_reply` has a findings branch that keeps only the claims (each read by the provider's `read_claim`: with Bedrock the quote must be word for word in the saved source and every figure in the claim in the quote), sets an empty paragraph, no links and `headline` to the section title, and discards a reply with no claim ("returned no findings"). At most 5 searches and 3000 output tokens a call; a Bedrock reply cut off at the cap skips the section with its own sentence. The whole title list is in the prompt so sections do not repeat each other. A section with nothing found is an `unavailable` block under its heading.
  - **No lead, no conclusion.** The two `complete()` calls that wrote them are gone, and so are their checks (a closing title gets no block; it appears in the contents list). `checks.check_piece` still handles a saved older Focus's lead, section and conclusion roles.
  - **In the review.** Each section is a narrative block with `role: "findings"`, empty `body`/`body_md`, its claims and `analyst_note` = `GUIDE_NOTE` ("Research notes for the analyst to write this section from. They are not report text and are not exported..."). `BlocksPane.vue` shows the claims as a list of research notes (claim, source link, verified quote), never as a paragraph to approve. Each claim is still a review item whose figures are checked against its quote, and a mismatch is shown in the item's text, but **nothing in a findings Focus is ever flagged** (every block the Focus builds carries `guide: true`, which `check_section` and `build_review` read: its claims and unavailable sections are not-auto-verified items, its carried disclaimer has no item), so approving it needs no acknowledgement and it can never hold the export; readiness and export status read only the section's decision. A findings claim has "Remove this finding" but no "Edit this sentence" (that needs a flagged item). The contents list (built by code from the titles, `role: contents`) and the disclaimer (carried **verbatim** from the previous issue's Focus, `role: disclaimer`; `unavailable` with "Paste the disclaimer in by hand" when it cannot be read) are kept visible as aids; neither is exported for a findings Focus. The section carries `outline`, `model_calls` (the outline call plus one per searched section), `api_requests` and `pieces_expected` = the number of searched sections.
  - **In the export.** A Focus with any `findings` block prints, under its Heading 1, one bold red line `Focus of the Week: analyst to write` (`export.GUIDE_LINE`) and nothing else: no findings, contents or disclaimer. The file name still comes from the typed topic. A findings Focus has no summary (`section_summary` returns ""), so the Executive Summary has no Focus line.
  - **Old reviews (rule 12).** A saved single-piece Focus (one headline and one body, no `role`) and a saved Focus with a model-written lead, sections and conclusion open and export exactly as before: every new branch is keyed on `role == "findings"`, which only the new shape has (`focus_title` skips role blocks; tests use `section_helpers.old_focus_review`).
