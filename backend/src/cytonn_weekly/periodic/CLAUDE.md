## Report types (verified 2026-10-05; the Monthly 2026-11-03)

Read from the published issues themselves: cytonnreport.com's API (`/api/series/1/research-reports/<id>`, section names, bodies and `summary` fields) and the PDFs (pdfplumber text layer). Ids: weekly #38.2026 = 891, Q3'2026 = 892, H1'2026 = 877 (876 is an earlier duplicate), Q1'2026 = 859, FY'2025 = 841 (840 earlier). PDFs: `cytonn.com/uploads/downloads/cytonn-q32026-markets-review-vf.pdf`, `cytonn-quarterly-report-h12026vf.pdf`, `cytonn-2025-annual-markets-review-and-cytonn-weekly-012026vf.pdf`.

**What a Markets Review is.** It goes out in the Sunday Weekly slot instead of a weekly and carries that week's content too ("During the week, ..." in Fixed Income, the Kenyan Shilling, Equities, Real Estate and all of Digital Payments' highlights). Only Q1 and Q3 are quarterly reviews: the Q2 slot is the half-year review (H1) and Q4's is the annual review (FY). The page title carries no weekly number ("Cytonn Q3' 2026 Markets Review"), but the reviews still use up a number: #11.2026 is 22 Mar and #37.2026 is 20 Sept with every Sunday between numbered, Q1 (5 Apr) and H1 (5 July) included, so Q3 (4 Oct) is presumably #39.2026 (confirm when the 11 Oct issue appears as #40). The FY'2025 review (4 Jan) is not numbered: #01/2026 is 11 Jan (the Q1 review cites it so), although the annual PDF's file name says "cytonn-weekly-012026". 18 Jan 2026 (id 843) is a separate markets-outlook issue (Global Markets / Sub-Saharan Africa Regional Market / Kenya Macro Economic with Cytonn's outlooks), not modelled. Framing: the period against the same period a year earlier ("163.9%, up from 110.6% in Q3'2025"), "q/q" and "the previous quarter" for quarter moves, "YTD performance" for the year; the website builds the "Executive Summary" from each section's `summary` field, and the PDF prints it first.

**The Cytonn Monthly** (`monthly`, stage 1, 2026-11-03; read from issues 845, 851, 864, 868, 881, 886 and 897 on cytonnreport.com, headings only). A Monthly takes the weekly's Sunday slot on the first Sunday after its month ends (the October 2026 Monthly is issue 897, 1 Nov 2026) and, like a Markets Review, carries that week's content too. Eight a year: no March, June, September or December, whose slots are the Markets Reviews. Six of the 2026 issues earlier counted as weeklies were Monthlies, so the fixture now holds 29 full weeklies and 6 Monthlies (35 full issues together). The "--N" at the end of a page address is the site's slug counter, not an issue number.

- **Sections, in 897's order:** Fixed Income, Equities, Real Estate, Digital Payments. No Focus of the Week and no Executive Summary for a Monthly. The registry's `subsections` hold 897's headings, with the parts not in every issue marked "optional" and named by issue; `charts` give each section's image count (Fixed Income 9, Equities 2, the others none) and say the titles are unknown, because no Monthly gives a chart a caption or alt text.
- **Label:** `Oct'2026` (stored with a straight apostrophe). `normalize_period` accepts "Oct'2026", "October 2026", "oct 2026", "Oct-2026", "oct/2026", a curly or spaced apostrophe and any case, and refuses anything else with "'x' is not a month; write it as Oct'2026". `period_window` is the month's first and last day; `year_ago` and `prose_label` work as for the reviews. `suggested_period` is the month before today's, or "" when that month has no Monthly.
- **Refused months** (`report_types.MONTHS_COVERED_BY`, the one table to change to relax the rule): the refusal reads "<Month> <year> has no Cytonn Monthly: it is covered by <review>. Choose <type>."

- **Previous-issue kind** (`weekly/previous_issue.py`): `Issue.kind` is now "review", "monthly" or "weekly". A Monthly is known by the search title "Cytonn Monthly" when the issue was reached through a pasted page address (`Issue.title`), else by what it prints (`MONTHLY_MARKERS`: the "Notable Monthly Highlights" heading, in every Monthly but 845, or the "Bond Issuances for <month> <year>-<month> <year>" table, in all seven; a review's is "Bond Issuances in <period>"). A review is still a review first. **The kind never chooses an issue**: the walk and the twin rule read only dates and image counts, so the same id comes back for all 37 replayed weeks (tested by serving every Monthly as a weekly). What changes is the wording: text carried from a Monthly holds `kind: "monthly"` in `carried_from` and its flag adds "; the text is from issue <id>, the Cytonn Monthly of <published>, not a weekly; check that it speaks of this week and not only of the month" (the weeks ending 6 Feb, 6 Mar, 8 May, 5 Jun, 7 Aug and 11 Sep 2026), and a skipped twin's sentence names "a Cytonn Monthly". This applies to the Fixed Income and Equities outlooks and the shilling text alike (`fixed_income/weekly._carried`).
- **Migration** (`db.py`). The report_type CHECK now lists six types (`db.REPORT_TYPES_SQL`), in `schema.sql`, `schema_postgres.sql` and the column the older migration adds. SQLite cannot change a CHECK, so `_allow_monthly_sqlite` (run by `_migrate`, inside its transaction) rebuilds `coordinator_reviews` from its own stored SQL with the list widened, inside a savepoint: every column, row and index, and the run id sequence, kept; a table that already allows 'monthly' is left alone. PostgreSQL's CHECK is unnamed, so `_allow_monthly_postgres` (inside the schema transaction) finds it in `pg_constraint` by schema, table and definition, and drops and re-adds it in one `ALTER TABLE` as `coordinator_reviews_report_type_check`. SQLite raises, so the app does not start, when the stored table text is not what it recognises; PostgreSQL does nothing when it finds no such check (nothing can then refuse a monthly row) and raises, so the app does not start, when it finds more than one. Rolling back is the same statement with the five-type list, and only while no monthly row exists.

**What is built for the Markets Reviews** (`periodic/`, one module per section, each with `STUBS` and `CHARTS`):

- **Fixed Income**: the "Bond Issuances in <period>" table from every CBK result of the period, exact against the Q3'2026 issue (Total 380.0 / 543.8 / 840.4; Average 12.8%, 13.5%, 221.2%, 64.7%; Q3'2025 Total 250.0 / 405.3 / 713.1). Rules found in the issues: switches listed (Q3) but never in the totals; buybacks never listed or counted; **tap sales counted** (checked against CBK's PDFs for five printed periods, 2026-10-06: counting them reproduces the Totals of Q3'2025, 250.0 / 405.3 / 713.1, in both the Q3'2025 issue, which lists the 25-08-2025 IFB tap as two "-Tapsale" rows, and the Q3'2026 issue that repeats them; of Q3'2024, 145.0 / 150.3 / 199.3 with two taps; and the offer and accepted amount of H1'2025, 335.0 / 464.1 with one tap. H1'2026 is the exception and is inconsistent with itself: its text says one bond was issued on tap sale, its table has no tap row, and its Totals of 460.0 / 510.0 / 781.1 are its nine primary auctions alone, where CBK lists two June 2026 tap sales and the tool's count is 495.0 / 547.6 / 820.9. The rule stays "counts tap sales" and the question stays open for the analysts); Average subscription and acceptance are ratios of the totals, tenor/coupon/yield plain means; the issue's "Issue Date" is mostly CBK's signing date and sometimes the value date, so the tool prints CBK's value date throughout; the issue's own figures sometimes disagree with CBK (the 26-08 switch's 22.5 vs CBK 21.8; SDB1/2011/030 yield 13.8% vs 13.694; the 13-07 auction's 178.3% vs 206.4%). A Total or Average built from fewer inputs than it names (a bond with no printed remaining life, an unread amount) carries an `incomplete` map on its source row, and `review/checks.check_table_block` flags each such figure ("average of 9 of 12 bonds: no years_to_maturity for ...") even though it equals its own derivation. If any auction of a period cannot be read, that period's Total/Average rows are flagged instead of summed from the rest. Plus cited highlights (MPC, government borrowing).
- **Kenya Macro**: "Major Inflation Changes – <month>" from KNBS's CPI release (Table 1, divisions named in KNBS's own overview, then Overall Inflation), exact against Q3'2026 (0.9%/9.5%, (0.4%)/15.6%, 0.1%/3.2%, 0.4%/6.8%); the issue's "Reason" column is asked of the inflation narrative. Cited narratives: GDP, inflation, shilling, monetary and fiscal policy (annual: balance of payments too).
- **Equities**: the afx index table (week and YTD) plus cited period highlights. **Real Estate**: the Ibuka REITs table plus a cited overview and the weekly themes over the period. **Global Markets**, **Sub-Saharan Africa**: cited narratives (World Bank and IMF first). **Digital Payments** (Q and H1 only): the weekly drafter's four highlights, the period stock table, and cited "other notable highlights". The table's period columns (`periodic/digital_payments.py`, checked cell by cell against the issues 2026-10-06): **quarterly**: Year Open, the previous quarter-end close, a week ago, the quarter-end close, current, w/w, **Q/Q** (the quarter-end close over the previous quarter-end close, minus 1; Q3'2026: 9/30 against 6/30), YTD, Forward P/E. On the captured Yahoo closes it reproduces every price and every w/w, Q/Q and YTD of the Q3'2026 issue for all seven companies, except Global Payments' Year Open and YTD (the known difference). The **m/m** column is the one stub. **Half-year**: Year Open, a week ago, the 6/30 close, current, **HY change** (the 6/30 close over Year Open, minus 1), w/w, YTD, P/E, with no stub. The H1'2026 issue printed its HY change and its w/w with the ratio inverted (see Open items), so the tool's HY change differs from that issue by design: the review carries one warning saying so (`HALF_CHANGE_WARNING`, also the column's `note`), and `KNOWN_PRINTED_ERRORS` is the single documented exception that keeps those two printed columns from ever being used as a test target. The tool's figures are still exact-matched against their own source. A price column is headed with the date of the close it holds: the issue's "Price 10/03/2026" (a Saturday) holds the Friday 2 October closes, so the tool heads it "Price 10/02/2026"; H1'2026's "Price 7/3/2026" holds the 2 July closes (3 July was a market holiday). The week-ago price is the weekly fetcher's rule, the close on or before seven days before the latest close: for H1'2026 that is 25 June where the issue used 26 June (not changed; to confirm).
- **Executive Summary**: composed only once every other section of the same report and period is approved; it carries each approved section's lead narrative piece verbatim (claims, `drafted_by` and dev-mode mark included) and re-checks its figures. No LLM. The review says what it is (`summary_note`: "Composed from the lead piece of each section. It does not summarize tables, parts not yet drafted, or Cytonn's own outlook.") and lists, per included section, what the approved review holds that the summary leaves out (`not_covered`: each table by title, each unavailable part by name, each further narrative piece by headline), shown as an info Notice and a dashed panel in `BlocksPane.vue`. Compared with the real Q3'2026 Fixed Income summary (614 words across T-bills, bonds, the week's auction and Cytonn's MPC view), the composed line is one cited piece (120 to 260 words by brief) on one theme.
- **Charts**: not generated. Each section records the real issue's chart titles (`CHARTS`, verbatim; Q3'2026 has 23 chart images: Global 2, SSA 3, Kenya Macro 5, Fixed Income 8, Equities 2, Real Estate 3); the start screen lists them and the review screen shows them as a "Charts to add by hand" checklist note with the issue they come from. Uncaptioned charts are described as such, never given an invented title.
